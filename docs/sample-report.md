# A Field Guide to Better Reports

An example editorial report, designed to make structured information enjoyable to read.

## Executive summary

A useful report does three things: **clarifies what happened**, explains why it matters, and gives the reader a sensible next step. A clear hierarchy is often more valuable than elaborate visuals.

> Make the important information effortless to find, and the nuanced information worthwhile to read.

### Key findings

- **Clarity beats density.** Short paragraphs and focused headings improve navigation.
- **Consistency builds trust.** Repeated styles help readers understand the structure.
- **Good typography matters.** Comfortable line lengths make long reading sessions easier.

## Recommendations

1. Begin with a plain-language summary.
2. Give every major section a descriptive heading.
3. Use lists for actual lists, not every sentence.
4. Add a clear conclusion that identifies your next action.

## Example comparison

| Element | Less effective | More effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heading | General updates | Project milestones |
| Paragraph | Twelve dense lines | Three focused sentences |
| Conclusion | Summary | Decision and next step |

## Conclusion

The strongest reports do not make the reader work to understand them. They make thoughtful use of language and the space around it.
